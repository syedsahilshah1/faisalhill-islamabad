Overview of the purchase process

Buying a plot in Faisal Hills involves three broad stages: finding the right plot, verifying it's legitimate, and completing the legal transfer. Each stage has specific steps and potential pitfalls. This guide walks through all of them clearly.

The entire process from first contact to transfer typically takes 2–6 weeks, depending on how quickly both parties produce documents and how busy the scheme's transfer office is.

Step 1 — Find a verified listing on the map

Start by opening the Faisal Hills interactive map and filtering for your desired block, plot size, and budget. The map shows all current listings as pins on the exact block layout — so you can immediately see whether a plot is in a location you want.

When you find a candidate listing:

  • Check the block and street details — corner plots or main boulevard-facing plots command a premium
  • Note whether it says "possession in hand" or "file only" — this matters enormously
  • Click the WhatsApp link to open a direct conversation with the dealer

You can also browse all listings in list view or filter by 5 marla or 10 marla to narrow your search.

Step 2 — Contact the dealer and negotiate the price

Use the WhatsApp link from the listing to contact the dealer directly. In your first message, confirm:

  • Is the plot still available?
  • Is this a possession plot or a file plot?
  • What utilities are connected (electricity, gas, water)?
  • Is the price negotiable, and what is the final cash price?
  • Can the seller provide original documents for verification?

Check current market rates on the plot prices page before negotiating — it shows typical ranges so you don't overpay or walk away from a genuine deal.

Step 3 — Visit the site in person

Never commit to buying without physically visiting the plot. During the site visit:

  • Confirm the plot boundaries match the file documents and are physically demarcated
  • Check road access — is the street complete or still under development?
  • Look for electricity poles and gas pipeline markers on the street
  • Talk to neighbours — they often know the history of the plot and any disputes
  • Take photos and note the plot number visible on any boundary markers

Use the satellite view on the Faisal Hills map to cross-reference what you see on the ground with the layout plan overlay.

Step 4 — Verify the documents at the scheme office

This is the most critical step. Before paying anything, go with the seller to the Faisal Hills management/scheme office and:

  • Ask the office to confirm the allotment letter or transfer deed is genuine
  1. Verify the seller's CNIC matches the name on the documents
  2. Check for any outstanding dues or encumbrances on the plot
  3. Confirm the plot number and block match exactly
  4. Ask about the NOC status for that specific block

Warning: Never rely on photocopies or photographs of documents. Always see originals and have the scheme office verify them. File fraud — where someone sells a plot using duplicate or altered documents — is a real risk in Pakistani housing schemes.

Step 5 — Agree on payment terms and sign the agreement

Once documents are verified and you're satisfied with the plot:

  • Agree on the final price and payment breakdown (any instalments if applicable)
  • Both parties sign a written sale agreement (drafted by the scheme office or a local property lawyer)
  • Pay the agreed token amount (advance/earnest money) against a written receipt
  • Set a clear date for the full payment and transfer

Do not pay any amount without a written, signed receipt. The receipt should specify the plot number, block, agreed price, amount paid, and the remaining balance and due date.

Step 6 — Complete the transfer at the scheme office

On the agreed transfer date, both buyer and seller visit the Faisal Hills management office with:

  • Seller: original allotment letter or previous transfer deed, CNIC, 2 passport photos
  • Buyer: original CNIC, 2 passport photos
  • Payment: remaining balance (cheque, bank transfer, or cash as agreed)
  • Transfer fee: payable to the scheme management (varies by plot size)

The scheme office processes the transfer deed and issues a new allotment letter in the buyer's name. This is your legal proof of ownership — keep it in a safe place.

Step 7 — Check withholding tax obligations

Property transactions above a certain value threshold are subject to withholding tax under Pakistan's tax laws. The rates differ between tax filers and non-filers. Consult a local property lawyer or tax consultant to understand your obligations before completing the transfer — surprise tax liabilities can affect your effective purchase cost significantly.

Red flags — when to walk away

  • Seller refuses to visit the scheme office for document verification
  • Documents only available as photocopies or photos on a phone
  • Significantly below-market price with no logical explanation
  • Urgent pressure to pay immediately before full verification
  • Third-party "agent" handling everything without the actual owner being present
  • Plot number or block doesn't appear in the scheme's official records
